# D20: design

## Why

The Sender phone goes inside a 20-sided box, the same way it goes inside the cube for the die page. An observer watches the die page and sees a 20-sided die move with the box, with the number on top in big type. The app defines the numbering, and the user writes the numbers on the box to match.

## Goals

- A **D20** tab on the Viewer's stage, next to Phone and the cube. It shows a 3D icosahedron that moves with the Sender, and the number on top (1–20).
- A **D20** option on the Sender's QR switch. It opens the observer's die page with the D20 on it.
- A mounting rule and a numbering method the user can follow to build and number a real box.

## Non-goals

- Changing what the Sender streams or the protocol.
- Other dice (d4, d8, d10, d12).
- A printable net or numbering map. The app itself is the numbering guide (see below).
- Correct readings when the phone is mounted any other way than the rule says.

## Anti-goals

- Breaking anything that already exists. Die links that have been shared (`view=die`) and saved Viewer / Sender choices must still open the cube.
- A number on screen that differs from the face on top of the box when it rests on a face.

## Constraints

- One static file with no build step, and no new libraries.
- No protocol change, and all text written with `textContent`.
- The reading must not depend on alpha (spin about the vertical), the same as the cube.

## Approaches

1. **CSS 3D, like the cube (chosen).** 20 triangular faces (`clip-path`) inside the same `.model-3d`, placed with transforms. They rotate with the same transform and the same smooth transition, and the tests check them against the face table the same way they check the cube. Risk: faint seams between triangles in Safari, fixed with a slight overlap.
2. **An SVG drawing redrawn on every packet.** Real lighting, but it adds a second way of drawing and needs its own smoothing to avoid looking jumpy at 10 Hz.
3. **three.js.** A large new library for one shape. Ruled out by the no-new-libraries constraint.

The user left the how to us, so approach 1 is our call.

## Design

**Mounting rule (approved by the user)**
- The phone lies on its back against the inside of one face, with its top edge pointing at one of that face's corners.
- That face is **20**. Its opposite face is **1**. When the box rests on face 20, the phone lies flat with the screen up, and 1 is on top, as on the cube.
- In device axes: face 1's outward normal is +z and face 20's is −z. A corner of face 20, seen from above, lies along +y.
- Fit (our calculation): a phone the size of an iPhone 15 needs box edges of at least about 10.5 cm on the inside.

**Numbering**
- Opposite faces add up to 21.
- Low and high numbers are spread out like on a real d20, so no corner gathers only low or only high numbers. The exact layout is chosen in the plan.
- To number a box, mount the phone, stream to a Viewer on the D20 tab, rest the box on each face, and write down the number shown. Afterwards, check that all 20 numbers came up once.

**Top face**
- The same method as the cube. Take the up vector in device axes (the bottom row of the W3C rotation matrix, `[-cos β sin γ, sin β, cos β cos γ]`), and pick the face whose outward normal is closest to it.
- Hysteresis keeps the current face until another one is about 3° past the edge between them. Adjacent D20 normals are 41.8° apart, not 90° as on the cube, so the D20 needs its own constant: about 0.04, against the cube's 0.08.
- It uses the same calibrated pose as the model. It shows `—` before the first packet and dims while the stream isn't Live.

**Drawing**
- 20 light-coloured triangles, in the cube's palette, each with its number in dark text. 6 and 9 are underlined, as on real dice.
- A fixed shade per face, so neighbouring faces can be told apart.
- About the same on-screen size as the cube. On the die page it scales with the screen.

**Viewer tabs:** **Phone | D6 | D20**. The cube's tab is renamed D6. The saved values are `phone`, `die` and `d20` (`gyrolink_viewer_model`), so existing saved choices keep working.

**Sender switch:** **Viewer | D6 | D20** (`gyrolink_sender_link`: `viewer`, `die`, `d20`). The D20 link is `?room=CODE&view=d20#…`. The share title is "Die", and the QR caption and aria-label name the die page, as the D6 option does today. New keeps the choice.

**Die page with `view=d20`**
- The same as `view=die` in every way except that it forces the D20. That covers the join, presence count, status wording, wake lock, the "Die" title, no calibration, the settings-only screen when credentials are missing, and an ignored `view=d20` without a valid room being dropped.
- `view=die` keeps opening the cube.

**Tests** (`tests/harness.test.js`)
- D20 faces as drawn, parsed from `index.html`: 20 faces, opposite faces add up to 21, face 1 at +z, face 20 at −z, a corner of 20 along +y. The drawn faces match the face table.
- The big number matches the face the drawn D20 points at the camera, across many poses. It doesn't flicker near an edge, and Calibrate re-zeroes it.
- The D20 tab saves `d20`, and a reload remembers it.
- Sender: the D20 switch puts `view=d20` in the QR code and link, with and without credentials. It is remembered, kept across New, and shared with the title "Die".
- `view=d20`: the die page opens with the D20 forced and not saved, and the Sender counts it. Without a valid room it is ignored and dropped from the URL.
- The existing `view=die` tests pass unchanged.

## Left to the builder

- The exact number layout, within the rules above.
- Colours, shading, triangle seam overlap, number size and rotation on each face, and the model's size.
- Whether the face transforms are written by hand in CSS or generated from the face table, as long as the tests compare the drawn faces with the face table on their own.
